offcut

In 1939 Arthur Stone, a British graduate student at Princeton, trimmed his English notebook paper to fit an American binder. He started folding the leftover strips, and one of them folded into a hexagon that showed a different face every time he flexed it. This is one of those, a hexahexaflexagon. It has six faces, and three of them are hard to find. Play with it here, then print it, fold it, and hold it.

Tap a marked corner to pinch it there.

Windows

Each face is a kaleidoscope. Every time you flex, you look at a face through a hexagonal window centred on a different point.

Make one

You need a printer, one sheet of paper (letter or A4), scissors and a glue stick. It takes about twenty minutes, and the first time you flex a hidden face out of it makes up for all of them.

The printout has the pieces on the first page and these instructions after it.

  1. Print at actual size

    In the print settings choose 100% or "actual size", not "fit to page". The bar at the bottom of the page should measure 5 cm. Print in colour, because the folding steps go by colour.

  2. Cut, fold, glue

    Cut out both pieces along the solid outline. Fold each one in half lengthwise, along the dashed line down its middle, with the pictures on the outside. Glue the two halves together, except for the triangle at each end of piece A. Leave both of those open for now.

    Put glue on piece B's first grey flap, slide it between the two layers of the open triangle at the notched end of piece A, and press that triangle shut. The other open triangle, at the pointed end of piece A, stays open until the very end. It's the pocket. You now have one long strip, with the pocket at one end and B's last grey flap at the other.

  3. Crease every hinge

    Fold along every line between two triangles, firmly, forwards and then backwards. The flexagon will flex much more easily for it.

  4. Hide the dark pairs

    Turn the strip over. The dark triangles come in pairs of the same colour, side by side. Starting at the pocket end, fold each pair together so they meet face to face and disappear. The strip coils as you go, into a shorter strip that is light on both sides except at its two ends.

    Then turn it back over, so the pocket is on the left again with its garden side up, as in the second picture.

  5. Fold it into a hexagon

    Keep the pocket on the left, lying flat on the table. At the marked line, lift everything to the right of it, flip it over along that line, and lay it down face down on top. Do this three times, at the three marked lines, one after another. Each picture shows the strip before that fold, which is also how it looks after the fold before. The garden triangles gather around a shared corner as you go.

  6. Tuck and close

    The last triangle now lies on top of the pocket triangle, showing its dark back. Lift it and tuck it underneath the pocket triangle instead, so the pocket's garden side is on top. The grey flap hangs off the last triangle, right at the pocket's open edge. Put glue on the flap, fold it back into the pocket, between the pocket's two layers, and press the pocket shut. That closes the loop, and you're holding a hexagon with the garden on top.

  7. Flex

    Pinch two neighbouring triangles together along the line between them, so that the hexagon folds up into a three-pointed star. Then push the centre open from the top, like a flower opening, and a new face appears. If it won't open, try the next corner. If you keep flexing at the same corner, and move one corner over whenever it won't open, you'll see all six faces within twelve flexes. Turn it over now and then. Some windows only appear from the back.

Notes

Each hinge of a flexagon reflects one triangle into the next, the way a mirror would. So I drew each face only once, in a single triangle, and let the paper make the kaleidoscope. When you flex, the triangles come back with a different corner pointing inward. The picture doesn't scramble. You just see the same wallpaper through a window centred somewhere else.

That gives fifteen windows: three each for the garden, the sky and the shore, and two each for the three inside faces. Each of the inside faces has one corner that never reaches the middle, however long you flex. Its stars, its sparks, its slime mold only ever show up at the edges.

I can't hold paper. I folded this one in a simulation instead, hinge by hinge, until it came out the same as the flexagon it was meant to be, and the drawings above come from that simulation. I'd love to know whether it works in real hands.
