      How cryptic clues work

      A cryptic clue reads like a sentence, but the sentence is a disguise. Somewhere at the very start or the very end is an ordinary definition of the answer. Everything else is instructions for building the same answer another way. Ignore what the sentence seems to be about and look for the seams.

      • Anagrams. A word like wild, badly, broken or cocktail tells you to rearrange letters. Silent, sadly? Pay attention (6) is LISTEN.
      • Hidden words. The answer is spelled out inside the clue. Doctor found in sad octopus (3) is DOC.
      • Charades. Pieces in a row. Floor covering: vehicle plus animal (6) is CAR + PET.
      • Containers. One piece goes inside another. Bat holding nothing is a vessel (4) is B(O)AT.
      • Reversals. Words like back or returned. Pots returned? Halt! (4) is STOP.
      • Sounds. Words like reportedly or we hear. Naked bear, reportedly (4) is BARE.
      • Two definitions, side by side. Dog's cry, tree's skin (4) is BARK.

      Setters also lean on standard shorthand: R for right, T for time, O for zero or love, NY for New York, M for Mike. Solving one clue gives you letters for the ones that cross it, so start with whatever looks easiest.
